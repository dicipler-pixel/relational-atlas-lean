## Plate XII — The spine (the one structural move)

The strata are not a filing order. They are a ladder, and each rung is built by the same move: **when a descriptor breaks, lift to the object that contains it.** What is lost at every step is local detail. What is bought is survival through the break.

The lifting ladder, bottom to top:

| Rung | Object | What it survives |
|---|---|---|
| eigenvector v | — | collapses at an exceptional point |
| Riesz projector Π | lift of v | survives it: the cluster is constant |
| Grassmannian bundle | lift of Π | survives eigenvector exchange entirely |
| QΠ = g − (i/2)Ω | quantum geometric tensor | survives the choice of basis |
| global ledger Z | regularised ledger | survives everything, checks against nothing |

- **[T]** The move is always the same. If a lower descriptor becomes singular or defective under deformation, and the next one up is built from it by a continuous canonical lift, the higher one stays well defined across the break. Every stratum boundary in this atlas is an instance.
- **[T]** And it is measurable. Subspace persistence exceeding single-vector persistence is not a metaphor for the ladder; it is the ladder, read off data: a rank-4 projector holding overlap where the leading eigenvector has already lost it.
- **[!]** The warning is at the top, not the bottom. Persistence climbs the ladder and so does distance from anything that can contradict you. The regularised ledger survives every deformation, which is precisely the trouble, since nothing can disturb it and so nothing can correct it either. Treat height as exposure, not as safety. "Durability is not evidence."

## Plate XVII — The method ("the same handful of moves")

Every result on the preceding sheets came out of the same handful of moves. They are collected here as one section rather than scattered as asides, and each is followed by what it cost or caught. A rule with nothing behind it is a preference.

The gate every other rule hangs off: **what result would have falsified it?** If nothing could have, it is a restatement (a literal typed into its own source, an identity wearing two names, two routes sharing one implementation), however many digits it agrees to. If something could have, it is a check: the positive control fires, the negative control stays silent, and the run could have come back no.

1. **[T] Ask what would have falsified it.** If nothing could have, it is a restatement however many digits it agrees to. Four instances in one week: a script whose every number was a string in its own source, a proportionality that was an identity, an algebraic rearrangement presented as a derivation, and two routes that were the same route.
2. **[H] Check prior art and circularity before investing, not before publishing.** An afternoon against a programme. And ask whether the constant being searched for is already in the construction; if the target sits inside the construction, the relation returned is the input restated.
3. **[H] Ask whether a symmetry forces the invariant to vanish.** Three programmes died on this: a boundary term identically zero in even dimensions, a Hermitian connection on a real family with simple eigenvalues, a curvature identically zero on a level set. The repair each time was the object adapted to the asymmetry: biorthogonal, winding, block.
4. **[H] Build the confound out of the model rather than arguing about it afterwards.** Fixing the spectral gap by construction makes any degeneracy that appears geometric by construction; computing every rung twice in bases related by a random unitary turns coordinate-freedom from a claim into a measurement; a feature removed by a small generic perturbation is measure-zero and not a phenomenon, though the converse does not hold, since a perturbation can also unfold a single degenerate point into a family that then persists, so the count is taken after it and not before.
5. **[H] Reproduce a known value before anything downstream, and stop if it fails.** A pipeline that returns nothing for a wrong input is behaving correctly: a null result indicts the input before it indicts the method.
6. **[H] Separate the terms before summing, and the routes before agreeing.** An anomalous exponent is either an intrinsic smallness or a cancellation between larger terms, and only the separated terms say which. Agreement between two routes that share an implementation is worth nothing; three estimators sharing no machinery is what makes a number credible.
7. **[H] Diagnose the correction form, tare the baseline, name the variable.** Never extrapolate blind: establish that the correction settles first. Subtract the known baseline before calling a spread an artifact. And the same singularity carries different exponents in different variables; at a square-root point, one in the distance and one in the gap differ by a factor of two.
8. **[H] Validate the instrument, name the wall, quote the systematic.** Never compare a quantity across models until the estimator has passed a control in each. When something stops improving, say whether precision, compute or physics is binding. And when a fit carries a shape systematic, let the window spread be the error bar: one ladder fit read ±0.00015 statistical against ±0.009 across six windows.
9. **[H] Repair rather than delete, and publish the kills at full size.** The default when something looks wrong is that it is real but misstated: most often the conclusion is right and the derivation, number or label is at fault.

## Plate XVIII — Tip to tail (the invariance ladder as a measurement)

One system computed twice, rung by rung, in two bases related by an arbitrary unitary. The question at each rung is only whether the choice survived. Six lifts from a matrix whose every entry was arbitrary to a whole number; nothing was added along the way, choices were removed one at a time.

| Rung | Two bases | Did the choice survive |
|---|---|---|
| Σ the operator | entries differ by 3.0 | basis dependent |
| Π the projector | Tr Π = 2.000000000000 · 2.000000000000 | first invariant, and an integer |
| ∂Π the deformation | ‖X‖ agrees to 5×10⁻¹¹ | invariant |
| g how far it moved | 0.355095622544 · 0.355095622552 | invariant |
| Ω which way it turned | 0.383302468810 · 0.383302469039 | invariant |
| C the integer | 0.9999984836, distance to one 1.5×10⁻⁶ | quantised |

And what the ladder cannot see: rungs four and five look like a matched pair, and are not. det g: 5.17×10⁻⁵ against 5.76×10⁻⁵; Ω: +0.014383 against −0.015181. Distance is blind to orientation; holonomy is not (B71).

## Plate XIX — Standing rules of the survey

The atlas is a method before it is a map. These are the rules that produced everything on the preceding sheets, and the one convention that must be stated wherever the curvature first appears.

- **I.** No arrow without a statement, and no statement without a grade. An unlabelled connection is the failure mode this atlas was built to end.
- **II.** Check for prior art before investing in a result, not after. A two-hour search has killed claims that would otherwise have reached a paper.
- **III.** Repair before deleting. A failed check usually means a right conclusion carrying a wrong derivation, a mislabel, or a missing factor.
- **IV.** Every check must be argued before it is run. Say what the result would prove, then run it, so a flawed test is caught before its output is believed.
- **V.** A conclusion reached five ways may be one derivation repeated. Find the step they share and audit that.
- **VI.** Grades may rise and fall, and a statement that drops carries its new grade here rather than disappearing. A claim withdrawn outright leaves this sheet, and the reason it went is kept in the working ledger. The grade is the honesty on this document; the ledger is the honesty behind it.

## The rule-card contract (atlas calculus note, 7 September 2026)

The calculus note proposes that the Atlas become a rulebook whose cards carry six things:

1. **Object and interface:** what enters, what leaves, and which coordinates or labels remain accessible.
2. **Allowed move:** the actual algebraic or diagrammatic replacement.
3. **Retained data:** response, scalar weight, orientation, multiplicity, phase or other specified quantities.
4. **Readout:** the exact question being preserved, including the threshold and pairing.
5. **Conditions and error:** a domain certificate and, for approximation, a composition-compatible bound.
6. **Evidence:** a written proof or measured status, a runnable example, and a counterexample showing why a condition is needed.

The first exact rule in that language is the Schur elimination with retained determinant weight, (M, w) ↦ (S, w·det D), proved and executable for finite real symmetric positive-definite assembled matrices, with the worked counterexample showing why the retained scalar cannot be dropped. The repository card schema (`atlas/cards.json`: statement, assumptions, failure mode, evidence status, verification scope, credit, sources, next step) is the same contract in catalog form.
